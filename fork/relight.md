# Relight

List: Relight, a Develop section with a light you drag on the photo that brightens what is near it in depth and faces it, from the photo's own depth map or an on-device depth model, live while you drag (issue #288), the ⌥-drag preview that shows where the light reaches (issue #294), up to eight lights, each with its own handle, size ring and colour (issue #299), a type for each light (issue #300), a gel, a pattern and haze for each light (issue #302), shadows from what the photo shows (issue #315), and light presets placed round the subject (issue #301). Branches `feat/288-relight`, `feat/294-relight-alt-overlay`, `feat/299-relight-several-lights`, `feat/300-relight-light-types`, `feat/302-relight-gels-patterns-haze`, `feat/315-relight-shadows`, `feat/relight-subject-presets`.

## What it does

- **Relight** section in Develop (between Lens Blur and Calibration): the list of lights (Light 1 … Light 8, each with its colour and Amount, a click selects one; **Add Light**, **Duplicate**, **Delete**), a small depth picture of the photo with every light on it (the selected one ringed), a Near–Far track for the selected light's depth, its **Amount**, **Softness**, **Warmth**, and an **Advanced** flyout with **Darken Surroundings** (the whole photo's) and the selected light's **Wrap**. While the section is open and the lights change the photo (some Amount or Darken Surroundings above 0, the section's eye on), every light's handle sits on the photo; one drag is one undo step.
- **Several lights (issue #299):**
  - **Up to 8 lights**, at least one (a new photo has one at Amount 0, as before). Each has its own place, depth, Amount, Softness, Warmth and Wrap; Darken Surroundings is one value for the section and darkens what the combined light doesn't reach. Delete on the only light puts it back to its defaults.
  - **Selection** is UI state (`Session::relight_selected`, one photo at a time, never saved in the edit, not an undo step; an index past the last light reads as the last). The sliders, the depth track and the shorthand controls act on the selected light.
  - **On the photo:** a press on a handle selects that light (a click on an unselected one only selects it) and a drag from it moves it; the selected handle has a ring (radius 24 points at Softness 0 to 64 at 100) with a grip: dragging the ring sets its Softness (one undo step, "Relight Size"); a scroll over a handle moves that light nearer (up) or farther by 5 of 100 per wheel notch, the rest of a slow trackpad scroll kept between frames (the view doesn't take that scroll; a run of scrolling is one undo step, closed after 0.4 s of quiet, and a scroll during a drag of a handle changes nothing; where handles overlap a press takes the one drawn on top, the selected one and then the later one; on the web build the handles and the scroll claim are inert: `detail/relight.rs`). In the depth box a press near a handle selects it.
  - **Adding up:** each light's `min(falloff · facing / peak, 1)` over the field is its **lobe** (`Field::lobe`; it does not depend on Amount, Warmth or Darken Surroundings), made once per light and kept (12 kept, most recently used first and a hit moves to the front, so every step of a drag finds the other lights' lobes; 6.8 MB each at 1600 × 1067: `pipeline::relight::LOBES`), so dragging one light makes one lobe, not eight. `pipeline::relight::forget()` frees every kept lobe and shading; the engine calls it when its field store lets a photo's field go, and the idle-memory work can call it too. The lobes add in scene-linear light, `ambient + Σ amountᵢ · lobeᵢ`, and the roll-off to the ceiling and the shadow hold work on that sum (so eight lights on one spot don't pass white); the combined shading (`Field::shading_of`) is kept too (4). Along depth edges the sum is refit against the photo like a single light's.
  - **Colour:** each light has a tilt (`warmth_tilt`). A pixel's colour is the mix of the lights' tilts, each weighted by the light it gave the pixel (`Σ addedᵢ · tiltᵢ / Σ addedᵢ`), times the new lights' share of its light. The finish stage's local temperature takes one plane per mask, so the colour goes up as two: one for the warm tilts (temperature the strongest warm tilt, each light weighted by its tilt over it) and one for the cool ones, which add where a warm and a cool light overlap. A photo with lights of one side only makes one plane, as before.
  - **⌥ view:** shows the combined light (one shading for all the lights), tinted by the lights' Warmth averaged by Amount; the selected light's handle keeps its ring above the dimming and its name ("Light 2") is drawn beside it.
  - **Old edits:** a block saved before this (one light's numbers and `darken` in one object) reads as one light and renders the same, byte for byte (checked by hand against the build before this change on ten renders: white, warm with Darken Surroundings, cool and tight, Darken Surroundings alone and warm and broad, each whole and cropped).
- **Light types (issue #300):** each light has a type, picked from the menu on its row in the list (a name and a chevron left of its Amount; a pick selects the light and sets `relight.<n>.kind`). A new light and every light saved before types are a **Point**, rendered byte for byte as before (the test `a_point_light_is_the_formula_it_always_was` compares its lobe with the old formula to the bit). The types' shading is in `crates/relight/src/kinds.rs` and only makes each pixel's `falloff · facing`; everything after it (the lobe, Amount, Warmth, the roll-off, the colour planes, the ⌥ view, CPU and GPU) is the point light's, so every type adds up with the others and renders alike everywhere. Positions are normalized photo coordinates, as the point light's.
  - **Spot:** the point light through a cone: its place (the handle), an **aim** (`spotAimX`, `spotAimY`: a second dot on the photo; the cone points at the surface under it, by the field's depth there), the cone's half-angle **Cone Angle** (`spotAngle`, 5–85°) and **Cone Edge** (`spotEdge`, 0 hard – 100 the whole cone fades). Drag the grip on the cone's edge to change the angle (on the photo the cone's lines are drawn at the angle itself, not foreshortened by depth). It keeps Softness (the source's size and fall-off), Warmth, Wrap and the Near–Far depth.
  - **Sun / Window:** one direction, no fall-off with distance, facing only: `sunAzimuth` (−180..180°: 0 from the camera's side, 90 from the right, 180 from behind the scene) and `sunElevation` (0° horizon .. 90° overhead). The handle is the arrow's base; the arrow points at the sun and its length is the height (the longest arrow is a sun overhead, a short one a low sun in front of the scene; at least 26 points drawn); drag its tip to set both (a drag keeps the sun in front of the scene: a hollow tip is a sun behind it, set by the slider). No depth: the section has no Near–Far control for a Sun / Window (nor for a Rim or a Fill). Softness makes the terminator gentler. A light that becomes a Sun / Window with Wrap still at its default starts at **Wrap 0** (surfaces turned away get nothing; `relight.<n>.kind` and `relight.addLight` do it, a saved file is read as saved); raise Wrap and they keep some. The Amount is what a surface that fully faces it gets (a fixed peak: no photo-wide normalization).
  - **Softbox:** a rectangle on the photo at the light's depth, facing into the scene (a surface nearer than it gets nothing from it): its middle is the handle, `boxWidth` and `boxHeight` (shares of the photo's width and height, 0.02–1.5) and `boxRotation` (degrees, clockwise). A corner grip resizes it and a knob above the top edge turns it. Each pixel sums the light of a 4 × 4 grid of points across it (each as a small round source, facing and cosine of the emitter), so a bigger box is softer and wraps more round shapes. No Softness.
  - **Rim:** no place (no handle); a thin bright edge along the near side of depth steps, the subject's outline (hair, ears, the edge of the face, shoulders), never patches inside a surface (issue #319). The main cue is the depth edge: a cone dilation of the log depth (`crates/relight/src/rim.rs`), so a pixel is lit by how far a farther surface lies across a step, and a step must be taller the farther it is: nothing under a step of 15 % of depth, whole from 57 %; a step counts as no more than 65 % behind the nearest thing round the pixel, so a deep backdrop gives no wider a rim than a near wall. It fades smoothly inward (squared, so it is an edge and not a band) over the reach, `rimWidth` (0–100: 0.4–4 % of the long side, 2.2 % at the default 50), is whole at the outline and follows the depth's edge (the hair's, with the joint-bilateral depth). Only the near side of a step is lit. The surface's turning away from the camera is only a weight (0.5 face on .. 1 edge on), so noisy normals never make a patch. Which side it comes from: `rimAngle` (−180..180°, 0 from above, 90 from the right, −90 from the left; 90 by default) and `rimSide` (0–100, one-sided: 0 the whole outline, the default and what a Rim saved before #319 has; 100 only the side facing the angle; an outline whose outward direction is square to the angle gets nothing at 100). At full Amount and the default width the lit edge gains about 2 stops (the soft ceiling). Fixed peak. No depth, no Wrap. The rim's map is made when its lobe is (not when the light is set up) and the lobe is kept like any other, so dragging another light does not make it again (a drag step with a Rim and a Point at 2000 × 1500: 36 ms before, 20 ms after, as the Point alone). A Rim saved before #319 (the type is new and unreleased) looks different: the pale patches are gone and the outline is cleaner.
  - **Fill:** no place; lifts the shadows by the luminance the photo shows (the guide's plus the photo's Exposure, as the grade reads it; the lobe is kept per Exposure): full in the deepest shadows (−6 stops under grey), fading linearly to nothing at Softness's top (0.5 to 2.5 stops: a little over mid-grey at the default 50), at 0.4 of the point light's strength so that full Amount lifts the deepest shadows of a face-on wall by about 1.5 stops and mid-grey by about 0.7 (a portrait's shadows by about 1.2 and 0.6, measured on p6 by exporting before and after). More on near things than far: its weight falls linearly with depth from the nearest to the farthest (`fillNear` 0 alike .. 100 the farthest gets nothing); from the camera's side (Wrap softens the facing). Fixed peak.
  - **Saved:** per light, `kind` (the type's name: `spot`, `sun`, `softbox`, `rim`, `fill`; left out for a Point) and each type's number that differs from its default: `spotAimX`, `spotAimY`, `spotAngle`, `spotEdge`, `sunAzimuth`, `sunElevation`, `boxWidth`, `boxHeight`, `boxRotation`, `rimWidth`, `fillNear`; fork 15 (#319) adds `rimAngle` and `rimSide`. The numbers of a type the light isn't stay with it, so switching back finds them. Read leniently (`KindSettings::read`): a kind this build doesn't know, or one that isn't a name or a number, reads as a Point; an index past the last reads as the last type; a number that isn't one is its default; every number stays in its range. Fork catalog format 14: a fork 13 build drops these fields and would open such a library as Points, so the format moved on. Fork catalog format 15: a fork 14 build drops `rimAngle` and `rimSide` and would show a one-sided Rim as a whole-outline one.
  - **Controls:** `relight.<n>.kind` (0 point, 1 spot, 2 sun / window, 3 softbox, 4 rim, 5 fill; a whole number), `relight.<n>.spotAimX`, `.spotAimY`, `.spotAngle`, `.spotEdge`, `.sunAzimuth`, `.sunElevation`, `.boxWidth`, `.boxHeight`, `.boxRotation`, `.rimWidth`, `.rimAngle`, `.rimSide`, `.fillNear`; the shorthands (`relight.kind` …) act on the selected light. The section shows only the selected light's own: Point Amount, Softness, Warmth; Spot those and Cone Angle and Cone Edge; Sun those and Sun Direction and Sun Height; Softbox Amount, Warmth, Box Width, Box Height, Box Angle; Rim Amount, Warmth, Rim Width, Rim Direction, Rim One-Sided; Fill Amount, Softness, Warmth, Near Bias. `relight.addLight` takes `kind?` (a name or 0..5); `relight.status` lists each light with its `kind` (omitted for a Point).
  - **On the photo:** a Rim and a Fill have no handle on the photo or in the depth box (select them in the list; ⌥'s picture names the light in the photo's corner). The size ring (Softness) is the point light's only. A drag of a type's handle is one undo step and ⌥ while it is held shows the combined light as for any control (`relight_alt`; `dragging_control` is the handle's control id). Own files: `panels/edit/relight/kinds.rs` (the menu, the controls by type), `panels/detail/relight/kinds.rs` (the handles).
- **Effects per light (issue #302):** an **Effects** flyout row after Advanced in the section, for the selected light. All drawn in code, no file read (`crates/relight/src/effects.rs`).
  - **Gel:** a colour for the light, any hue and a strength: five named gels as swatches (Warm Amber 38°/70, Teal 178°/65, Magenta 305°/65, Deep Blue 232°/75, Red 0°/75; `relight.setGel {gel}`, which also takes `hue` and `strength`, strength 0 takes it off) and **Gel Hue** (0–360°, 0 red, 120 green, 240 blue) and **Gel Strength** (0–100). Warmth stays: the light's colour is the gel's multiplied with Warmth's. The gel is `hue_colour ^ (0.8 · strength^1.6)` per channel (no change at 0, a strong colour at 1; a channel never goes under 0.12 of the light, so a gel stays finite). **How it reaches the render:** `Effects::light_colour` turns that colour into the finish stage's own local temperature and tint (`(1 + 0.3 t, 1 − 0.22 s, 1 − 0.3 t)`, luminance kept), the pair Warmth already used, so a gel goes up the same mask planes and the CPU and the GPU read one finish kernel and cannot differ. The colour planes grew from two (warm, cool) to four (warm, cool, magenta, green; `Shading::split`, ids `relight::IDS`), each made only when some light has that component, so a photo without a gel makes the same two planes as before. Lights mix as before: a pixel's tilt and tint are the lights' weighted by the light each gave it.
  - **Pattern:** the light shines through blinds, window panes, leaves or dapple (`relight.<n>.pattern` 1–4; 0 none), with **Scale**, **Rotation** (−180–180°) and **Softness**. The pattern is a projector's slide at the light: a surface point takes the pattern's value at the direction it lies from the light (`Field::pattern_plane`; tangent coordinates about an axis into the picture, out of it for a light behind most of the scene), so stripes run straight over a wall facing the camera, converge over a floor, jump at a step in depth and wrap a leg. A surface seen at a grazing angle to the light widens the edges (the depth model's normals); beside or behind the light's plane the pattern fades to its own mean coverage (half for blinds, 0.74 for panes, the baked texture's mean for leaves and dapple). Leaves and dapple are dear per pixel (scattered shapes), so each is baked once into a tileable texture (16 cells across, 24 texels a cell, one at its own softness and one with edges three times as wide for grazing surfaces; cached by pattern and softness, the newest four) and sampled bilinearly during the projection; blinds and panes stay analytic. Measured once by hand on a 1600 × 1067 field, release, one pattern plane: blinds 8 ms, panes 8–13, leaves 54 → 11, dapple 31 → 10 (the light's own lobe is another 7 ms). The pattern multiplies the light's lobe, so the facing term, the roll-off and the colour work as before; the patterned lobe is kept like the plain one (its key adds the pattern's numbers, none for a light without one). The shading's key hashes each light's effects with the light's place in the list, so the same effect on another light is another shading.
  - **Haze:** a soft glow around the light that grows with the distance in depth: `Field::haze_lobe` is a halo around where the light sits on the photo (radius from Softness) times a fog that is 0 at the nearest depth and 1 at the farthest. It is one more lobe of the same light and colour (`Part` with `HAZE_GAIN` = 0.25 of the light's strength at Haze 100), so it adds in the same sum, rolls off the same way and takes the gel. It brightens like the light does (an exposure on what is there), so it does not lift pure black.
  - **Old edits:** a light without effects saves exactly as before (the new keys are left out at their default) and renders to the byte as before (hashed twelve renders on #307's head and on this: white, warm with Darken Surroundings, cool and tight, Darken Surroundings alone, warm and broad, two lights, each whole and small).
  - **⌥ view:** shows the combined light, so the pattern, the haze and the gel's reach are in it (crosses cluster in the lit gaps; contours wrap them).
- **Shadows (issue #315):** a light is blocked by what the photo shows. A **Cast Shadows** slider (`relight.<n>.shadows`, 0–100, default 0) for a Point, Spot, Sun / Window and Softbox, the last control of the type's own: under Warmth for a Point, under Cone Edge for a Spot, under Sun Height for a Sun / Window, under Box Angle for a Softbox (a Rim and a Fill take no shadow: no slider); `crates/relight/src/shadow.rs`.
- **Light presets (issue #301):** a **Light Presets** flyout at the top of the Relight section lists the built-in presets (**Rembrandt**, **Butterfly**, **Split**, **Rim + Fill**, **Golden-Hour Window**, **Neon Two-Tone**, **Candle**; original setups) and then **My Presets**. Each row has a small picture of the current photo lit by it and says how many lights it sets; resting on a row previews it on the photo (no undo step), a click applies it (`relight.applyPreset {preset}`: the photo's lights and Darken Surroundings are replaced, the Relight eye is turned on, nothing else of the photo changes; **one undo step**). **Save Lights as Preset…** (on while some light is on) takes a name (`TextField`; Return or Save) and saves the photo's lights (`relight.savePreset {name}`); a row of your own has a menu with **Rename…** and **Delete Preset** (`relight.renamePreset`, `relight.deletePreset`); the built-in ones can't change; names are unique (letter case aside) across both lists, at most 40 characters, at most 100 of your own. `relight.presets {id?}` lists them and the subject.
  - **Placed round the face, at its depth:** a preset's lights are written for a face, not for a photo (`crates/develop/src/relight_presets.rs`, module docs): in the preset frame a face of 0.15 × 0.25 of the photo stands at the centre, and a key 0.4 of the photo's width to its left and 0.45 of its height above it is saved at `x = 0.1`, `y = 0.05`. Applying maps them onto the photo's face (`Subject::place`): a face twice as wide takes its lights twice as far from it, so the same preset fits a portrait, a group and a landscape. A spot's aim and a softbox's size scale likewise; a rim, a fill and every other number are carried as they are. **Depth** (a Point, Spot or Softbox): a light's depth is relative to the scene's nearest and farthest, so a preset writes it as a distance in front of the subject (`50 − 50·k` for `k` of log depth nearer the camera than the face, `DEPTH_SPAN` 2) and applying turns it into the photo's own depth value from the subject's: the median depth of the face's middle part in the photo's field (`Field::depth_in`, `crates/relight/src/probe.rs`) and the log depth 100 depth units cover there (`Field::light_depth_span`). So a key "at the face's distance" is at his distance whatever is behind him (a small man in front of far steps got the first version's lights on the steps, not on him). `relight.applyPreset` makes the photo's depth first when it isn't there (on this thread, like an export; without a depth model or map the depth is placed as written); thumbnails and the hover preview use it once it is there. Saving is the inverse (`Subject::capture`). The subject (`Session::relight_subject`) is the first of: the photo's face regions (the face scan's, else those portrait retouching found; the largest and any at least 0.4 of its area as one box), a face worked out from what a visible, not inverted person or subject mask selects (a face 0.38 of the box's width and 0.3 of its height, in its upper part), a face of the canonical size a little above the photo's centre. The face is kept between 0.08 × 0.12 and 0.4 × 0.6 of the photo.
  - **The built-ins** (original setups, each a recognisable picture in its thumbnail; Darken Surroundings is high in all, so the lit side and the shadow side differ at a glance): **Rembrandt**: a spot 45° to one side and above the face, close, aimed at it, with Cast Shadows 100 (the nose's shadow reaches the far cheek) and a 4 % fill. **Butterfly**: a spot centred and high (shadow under the nose and chin) and a soft fill from below. **Split**: one spot level with the face and well to the side, Wrap 0: half the face lit. **Rim + Fill**: a Rim all round the subject, a broad soft Point from the front (Softness 100, Wrap 100, no shadows) and a little Fill, with only Darken Surroundings 30: a Fill alone lifts only what is darker than its limit (and more on near things), so with the room darkened to 75 and a Rim giving the edge, the part of the cheek between them stayed at a quarter of its light, a dark stripe down the face (and a dark bar across a small face's forehead). **Golden-Hour Window**: a warm amber spot raking from the left across face and body, a weak cool fill on the right. **Neon Two-Tone**: a magenta spot from the left and a teal one from the right. **Candle**: one small warm point, low and close, with haze. None uses a Sun / Window: a Sun at a grazing angle lights the wall along the subject's outline (the depth model's soft depth steps tilt the wall's normals towards the sun) and leaves a pale halo there (found with Haze, Fill and Cast Shadows each switched off in turn: only the Sun's facing term draws it; Wrap 40 hides most of it but lights the whole wall).
  - **Stored:** your presets are in the library's `presets.json` (`relightPresets`), read leniently (no id or name, a repeated id or a built-in's id is dropped). No `ui.json` key and no develop setting is added; catalog format is unchanged (16). They are not in the settings file of Settings Sync yet (that carries the `.lcpreset` presets and `ui.json` only).
  - **How:** for each pixel's surface point, rays are walked through the depth map towards the light (to the light's place for a Point, Spot and Softbox, parallel along the direction for a Sun) and a ray is blocked where it passes through a **thing** (`crates/relight/src/occluder.rs`, `shadow.rs`):
    - **A thing is what has farther stuff on both sides of it** along some line through it (across, up and down, or a diagonal), within half the picture's long side: its outline is closed on that line. An outline is a step in depth (log depth at least 0.1 farther than the pixel and rising by 0.1 over two pixels), not the thing's own curve (the depth model makes a near face a quarter of its depth deep). A floor, a staircase and a wall seen at an angle recede one way and come nearer the other, so they never block, and neither do the depth model's ripples; a person in front of the steps beside them, a box, a pole, a car over the ground do. How sure that is grows smoothly with the step to the nearer background, from none at 13 % of the depth to full at 28 %, so nothing switches on at one depth.
    - **A thing is about as deep as it is wide:** it reaches behind its front by its narrowest width through the pixel (the shortest closed line, in scene units at its depth), never more than 70 % of the way to its background, and fades out over half that depth again (at most 90 % of the way). A pole is as deep as it is thick, a person as deep as their body is wide.
    - **The march:** a ray is followed on the reduced picture through every pixel it crosses (a thing one pixel wide is never stepped over), only over the depths where some thing lies, skipping each 4 × 4 block of pixels where nothing can hold it. At each pixel the range of depth the ray has there (exact: along a line on the picture, 1 / depth changes evenly) is held against the thing: its presence rises from 3 % behind its front to full at 8 % (or a quarter and a half of its depth for a thin thing), stays full to its depth and falls to nothing at its end. The pixel that holds back most decides the ray. So a thin thing still blocks in full, and a light or a surface moving in depth changes the shadow smoothly. A ray ends where it leaves the picture or reaches the light. A light inside a thing is hidden from everything; moving it out of the thing's back brings the light back over the fade.
  - **Soft edges from the light's size:** the rays aim across the light: a disc for a Point and a Spot (half its Softness radius, the sphere its fall-off comes from), for a Sun a disc of directions (angular radius 0.7° at Softness 0 to about 15° at 100), the rectangle itself for a Softbox. Four rays first (the middle and three round the edge); a pixel whose four rays differ by more than 0.05 is in a penumbra and gets twelve more (four rays agreeing on a partial value, a light hidden in part, are not refined). So a small light gives a hard shadow, a big one a soft one whose penumbra grows with the distance from the blocker. Each pixel turns its rays by a fixed noise that differs most between neighbours (interleaved gradient noise), and the visibility is averaged over the 3 × 3 neighbours on the same surface (log depth within 0.08), twice at full quality, so a wide penumbra is not grainy. An edge is one reduced pixel wide (4 pixels at 1600) where the shadow leaves the object, and then widens with the distance, by the geometry of the rays across the light.
  - **While dragging (draft):** the draft render (`Quality::Draft`, and the ⌥ view) marches coarser: a grid of about 200 pixels on the long side (8 field pixels to one at 1600), the first four rays only (no penumbra pass) and the nearest sample instead of the filter. The full march is made at the release and for every export. A draft takes a light's full lobe or visibility when it is kept (the other lights of the scene) and makes a coarse one, under its own key, only for a light that has none; the shading's key says draft only for a scene with shadows (`Scene::has_shadows`), so a scene without them shares one shading. The visibility memo keeps 24 (a full and a coarse one for each of eight lights and then some).
  - **Speed:** the march runs on a reduced copy of the field (`Reduced`, made once per field with what blocks in it: about 400 pixels on the long side, so 4 field pixels to one at 1600, 10–14 ms; 200 for a drag, 2–3 ms; a small field is not reduced), as a `Visibility` (1 lit .. 0 blocked) kept per light (24 kept, 0.4 MB each; `pipeline::relight::SHADOWS`, cleared by `forget()`). Its key is the field and only what the march reads for the type (a Point and a Spot: place, depth, Softness; a Sun: azimuth, elevation, Softness, no place; a Softbox: place, depth, the rectangle, no Softness): not Amount, not the shadow amount, not a Spot's cone, so the Shadows slider and every other light's drag don't march again. `Field::shadowed` brings it to the field's size with a joint bilateral upsample (four reduced samples, weighted by distance and by how near they are in the photo's brightness and log depth, weights on the photo's brightness and log depth that ignore differences under 0.08 and 0.05 (the photo's texture, ripples, a floor's own slope), so a shadow stops at a near thing's outline and its edge is not blocky) and multiplies it into the light's lobe as `1 − amount · (1 − visibility)`, after the pattern and before the lights add up. Darken Surroundings still darkens what the combined light misses. The lobe's key adds the shadow amount (only for a light that has some), so a light without shadows keeps the key and the lobe it had.
  - **Same everywhere:** the shadows are in the lobe, so the shading, the ⌥ view, the render and the export make one picture, and the GPU reads the same planes as the CPU (`lightcraft-gpu`: within 1 level).
  - **Measured by hand** on p7 (1600 × 1067, release, the example `relight` with `LC_SHADOWS=1`, `LC_DRAFT=1` for the draft; a point light behind the man at Softness 30; load average about 4.5; three runs): one drag step (the light's lobe, the march, the upsample and the shading that adds the lights up) is 15–18 ms as a draft (march 2.7–3.1 ms) against 12–16 ms with Cast Shadows 0. The full march at the release is 20 ms (upsample 3–10 ms). Making the reduced field and its blockers is once per photo (14 ms full, 2–3 ms draft). A drag of the Cast Shadows slider alone makes only the upsample and the lobe.
  - **Saved:** per light, `shadows` (0–100), left out at 0, so a light without shadows saves exactly as before and an old edit reads and renders to the byte as it did. Fork catalog format 16 (an older build refuses a library written in it, as for every format; it does not drop the key). Controls: `relight.<n>.shadows`, and the shorthand `relight.shadows` on the selected light; `relight.status` lists it.
- **Depth** is found off the UI thread by one worker (the newest request first, at most 12 waiting), shown as "Finding depth" in the top-bar activity ring, cancellable there: the photo's own depth map when it has one (an iPhone Portrait HEIC), else the depth model. It is kept in memory while it is among the most recently used (300 MB: a field holds up to 36 bytes a pixel, 52–92 MB at 1600 pixels, so 3–6 photos), so moving the light or editing the photo never finds it again; a photo pushed out of that set, or any photo after a restart, finds it again (nothing is saved to disk). Until it is ready the section shows Finding Depth…; without the model it offers Download Depth Model (asks once, then shows progress). A finding cancelled in the ring is not started again by renders; the section shows Find Depth (`relight.findDepth`).

## Rework (round 5, audits of PR #321)

Rounds 1–4 judged a blocker by a fixed band (1.08 to 1.5 times a surface's depth), a test along rows and fades at the band's edges; each fix traded one failure for another (the whole wall dark for a light just behind a subject, grey speckle on stairs, a jump at 1.5 times, a lost shadow for a light in front, shadows a third paler). The rework replaces all of it with the two rules and the stretch march above:

- **What blocks** is decided per pixel from the depth alone (farther stuff on both sides, so floors, stairs and walls never block), not per ray from where it starts.
- **How deep** a thing is comes from its own width, not a share of its depth: a light 20–30 % behind a person is behind them, not inside a 50 % band.
- **No paleness:** a ray is held over its whole range of depth at every pixel it crosses, so a ray that crosses a thing blocks in full (round 4 left a third of the light; a thin thing's shadow vanished). Fix round 6: the first rework looked up one point per two-pixel stretch, so a pole two pixels wide was stepped over by part of the rays (its shadow's darkest per row 0.33–0.53, and 0.42–0.67 while dragging); now 0.25–0.27 and 0.42–0.48 (test `a_thin_poles_shadow_is_whole_along_its_length`; a two-pixel pole with the smallest light has no umbra, so it never reaches 0.1).
- Each goal's test fails on an earlier round: the stepped floor stays lit and the wall keeps its light for a backlight (round 1); the floor in front stays in the person's shadow as the light moves through 1.3–1.9 times their depth (round 2); a surface just behind the subject changes smoothly with its depth (round 3); the wall is shadowed for a light in front of or level with the subject (rounds 1–4); a box's and a pole's shadow keep at most a tenth of a small light (rounds 1–4).
- Measured on p7 with a point light just in front of the man's depth, moved by 0.5 % of the width: no pixel's visibility changes by more than 0.1 at the 99.9th percentile; moved by 0.02 of the depth track, the shadow grows smoothly (mean visibility 0.802, 0.799, 0.796) with no line.

## Known limits

- Shadows are a march through one picture's depth: nothing is known behind a thing's front (a thing is taken to be as deep as it is wide), outside the picture, or in the hair and fine strands the depth model smooths over. The geometry is the depth model's: on p6 it puts the studio backdrop about six times farther than the man, so a sun throws his shadow small and far to the side, and a lamp near him hardly reaches the backdrop. A low sun from the side throws a shadow out of the picture. Things narrower than about two reduced pixels (8 at 1600) can be missed.
- A thing standing on a surface does not block near where it touches it (it stands out of nothing there), so a contact shadow fades out over the last part; a thing whose sides are as far as itself (a person leaning on a door frame, a counter as wide as the picture) is not a thing on that line, and blocks only if another line through it is closed.
- A blocker shades its own far side (the right cheek for a light on the left) as well as the facing term does; along its outline that edge can be a little grainy for a big light.

- A spot's hard edge (Cone Edge 0) leaks a little onto steep, evenly toned walls: the edge-band refit (a guided filter against the photo's luminance along depth steps) mixes the two sides' light there. By design; a soft edge hides it.
- A Rim follows the depth model's edges: on a staircase it also lights step edges; it is cleanest with a subject against a plain background.
- Haze sits at the light's place, so a Rim or a Fill (no place) takes none.
- A pattern swaps sides when a light's depth crosses the scene's mean depth: the aim into or out of the picture is picked by comparing the light's depth with the scene's mean (`Field::pattern_plane`), so the pattern jumps there (redo in #316).

## What's New entry

### Relight
- Light Presets: one click sets up several lights round the person in the photo. Seven built-in looks (Rembrandt, Butterfly, Split, Rim + Fill, Golden-Hour Window, Neon Two-Tone, Candle), each with a small picture of your photo; rest the pointer on one to see it on the photo. Save Lights as Preset… keeps your own.
- A new Develop section, Relight, adds a light you place on the photo. Drag its handle and the light reaches what is near it in depth and faces it, live while you drag. Amount, Softness and Warmth shape it; Darken Surroundings and Wrap are under Advanced.
- Relight holds up to eight lights: Add Light, Duplicate and Delete under the list, click a row or a handle to pick one, drag the ring around the picked handle to change its size and scroll over a handle to move it nearer or farther. The lights add up, each with its own colour, so a warm key and a cool fill work together.
- Each light has an Effects group: a gel (five named ones, or any hue and strength) that colours its light, a pattern (blinds, window panes, leaves or dapple) it shines through, laid over the surfaces so it bends over steps and faces, and haze, a soft glow that grows with distance.
- Lights cast shadows: a Cast Shadows slider under Warmth (0 by default) makes the things in the photo block the light, so a person between the light and the steps leaves a shadow on them. A small light gives a hard shadow, a big one a soft one.
- Hold ⌥ while dragging a Relight control to see where the light reaches: small crosses lying on the surfaces, or lines of equal light (a switch under the Near–Far track picks one).
- Depth comes from the photo's own depth map (iPhone Portrait photos) or from an on-device depth model, downloaded once (about 1.3 GB). Saved with the edit; like masks and spots it stays on its photo unless you tick Relight in Copy Settings or a preset. The export matches the preview. The web version has no Relight.
